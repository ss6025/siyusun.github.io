✻ OUR QUESTION

Half of the human genome is repetitive.

We ask what it remembers.

Long treated as genomic noise, repetitive DNA is a dynamic regulatory system. When its control breaks down, it can alter cell fate, activate immune pathways, and accelerate disease.

RESEARCH THEMES THREE CONNECTED SCALES
01

Repetitive genome

We develop computational approaches to resolve the hidden, repetitive regions of the genome and measure when they become active.
02

Cellular identity

We study how transposable elements and repeat-derived programs reshape cell state, plasticity, and immune signaling.
03
{ }

Disease evolution

We trace these programs across cancer, aging, and neurodegeneration to reveal shared mechanisms and new therapeutic opportunities.
